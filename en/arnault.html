Context: luxury in a slump

A tough year. Luxury demand is slowing, in China and elsewhere, and LVMH shares fell sharply in 2026, shrinking his fortune.

Succession. At 77, he can stay on as boss until 85 thanks to a change in the company bylaws. His five children hold key roles in the group.

Biography

Early years

Born in Roubaix into an industrial family, he graduated from Polytechnique in 1971 and joined the family construction firm, Ferret‑Savinel.

The Boussac deal

In 1984 he bought the bankrupt Boussac group, owner of Christian Dior — the starting point of his empire.

LVMH

In 1989 he took control of LVMH, formed by the merger of Louis Vuitton and Moët Hennessy, and grew it through acquisitions.

Family

Father of five — Delphine, Antoine, Alexandre, Frédéric and Jean — all working in the group.

His empire

Fashion & leather goods

Louis Vuitton, Dior…

The heart of the group and its most profitable division: Louis Vuitton, Christian Dior, Celine, Loewe, Fendi.

Wines & spirits

Moët Hennessy

Champagnes (Moët & Chandon, Dom Pérignon, Veuve Clicquot) and Hennessy cognac.

Other businesses

Tiffany, Sephora…

Jewellery (Tiffany, Bulgari), watches, cosmetics, the Sephora chain and media (Les Échos, Le Parisien).

Controversies

His economic and media power is regularly debated in France.

  • Belgian citizenship — In 2012 his application for Belgian citizenship was seen as an attempt to avoid tax; he withdrew it in 2013.
  • Squarcini case — LVMH admitted using a former French domestic intelligence chief for spying missions, which led to a trial.
  • Press — As owner of several media outlets, he banned his managers from speaking to some newspapers he considered hostile.
  • Taxes — He publicly opposes plans for a tax on the very largest fortunes in France.

A divisive figure

What admirers highlight

  • Built the world’s No. 1 luxury group, a flagship of the French economy.
  • Tens of thousands of jobs and preserved craftsmanship.
  • Major philanthropy (Fondation Louis Vuitton, Notre‑Dame de Paris).

What critics point to

  • Concentration of wealth and influence, including over media.
  • Accused of tax optimisation.
  • Family succession seen as opaque.
