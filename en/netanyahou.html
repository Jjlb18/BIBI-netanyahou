Background: the 2026 election

Election of 27 October 2026. Likud came first as the largest party, and Netanyahu's bloc won 53 of 120 seats, against 48 for Gadi Eisenkot's opposition bloc — not enough to govern alone.

8 November 2026. After consultations, President Isaac Herzog gave the mandate to form a government to Gadi Eisenkot (Yashar party), recommended by 67 MPs against 46 for Netanyahu. Eisenkot has 28 days, extendable by 14.

Meanwhile, Netanyahu remains caretaker prime minister and is pushing for a national unity government. If Eisenkot fails, the mandate could pass to him.

Situation as of 11 November 2026 — may change quickly.

Biography

Youth and army

Born in Tel Aviv, the son of historian Benzion Netanyahu, he spent part of his youth in the United States. From 1967 to 1972 he served in the elite Sayeret Matkal unit, taking part notably in the 1972 rescue of the hijacked Sabena flight.

His brother, Yoni

His older brother Yonatan (“Yoni”) was killed in 1976 while commanding the Entebbe raid in Uganda. The event deeply shaped his path and his commitment against terrorism.

Studies and diplomacy

An MIT graduate, he worked briefly in business consulting. He then became deputy chief of mission at Israel's embassy in Washington (1982), and ambassador to the UN (1984–1988), where he became known for his media skills in English.

Entering politics

Elected to the Knesset in 1988, he became Likud leader in 1993 and in 1996, aged 46, the youngest prime minister in the country's history.

His terms as prime minister

1996 – 1999

First term

Directly elected after the assassination of Yitzhak Rabin. Signed the Hebron and Wye River agreements with the Palestinian Authority, then lost to Ehud Barak.

2009 – 2021

Twelve straight years

Marked by opposition to the Iran nuclear deal, the US embassy move to Jerusalem (2018) and the Abraham Accords (2020). Ousted by the Bennett–Lapid coalition.

Dec 2022 – today

Return to power

Coalition with ultra-Orthodox and far-right parties; contested judicial overhaul, the 7 October 2023 attack, wars in Gaza, Lebanon and against Iran.

The corruption trial

Indicted in 2019, Netanyahu has been on trial since 2020 in three separate cases, all of which he denies and calls a “witch hunt”:

  • Case 1000 — luxury gifts (cigars, champagne) received from wealthy businessmen.
  • Case 2000 — alleged dealings with the publisher of the daily Yedioth Ahronoth to obtain favourable coverage.
  • Case 4000 — alleged regulatory benefits for the Bezeq group in exchange for favourable coverage on the Walla website.

In late 2025 he asked President Herzog for a pardon. The trial was continuing in 2026, with several hearings a week.

A divisive leader

What his supporters highlight

  • A firm security stance towards Iran, Hamas and Hezbollah.
  • Normalisation with several Arab countries (Abraham Accords).
  • A close relationship with the United States, especially Donald Trump.
  • Economic growth and liberal reforms as finance minister.

What his critics hold against him

  • Political responsibility for the failures of 7 October 2023.
  • The conduct of the Gaza war and its humanitarian toll; the ICC arrest warrant.
  • A judicial overhaul seen as a threat to the balance of power.
  • Staying in power despite a corruption trial; alliances with the far right.
