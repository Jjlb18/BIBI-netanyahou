Background: the “Epstein files”

November 2025. Congress passes, with support from both parties, the Epstein Files Transparency Act, requiring the Justice Department to release its files on Epstein and Ghislaine Maxwell.

December 2025 – January 2026. A first partial release, then on 30 January 2026 more than 3 million pages, 2,000 videos and 180,000 images, often heavily redacted to protect victims.

The documents mention many well-known people; being named is not proof of wrongdoing. Democrats say more than 2.5 million pages remain unreleased and want to continue the inquiry in 2027.

Situation as of 11 November 2026 — may change.

Biography

Origins

Born in Brooklyn to a modest family, he studied at Cooper Union and then New York University without graduating. He then taught maths and physics at the Dalton School, a private school in Manhattan (1974–1976).

Finance

He joined the investment bank Bear Stearns, then founded his own wealth-management firm for very rich clients. His main client was billionaire Les Wexner, owner of Victoria's Secret.

A powerful network

He owned homes in Manhattan, Palm Beach, Paris and New Mexico, and a private island in the US Virgin Islands. He mixed with politicians, scientists, members of royal families and billionaires.

Ghislaine Maxwell

His girlfriend and later close associate, daughter of press baron Robert Maxwell. She was convicted in 2021 of helping him recruit and exploit underage girls.

The case in brief

2005 – 2008

Florida

Palm Beach police investigate after a family's complaint. A highly controversial deal with federal prosecutors spares him federal charges: he pleads guilty in a Florida court and serves 13 months with work release.

2018 – 2019

Reopening and death

A Miami Herald investigation revives the case. Arrested in July 2019 for sex trafficking of minors, he dies in his cell on 10 August 2019; the medical examiner rules it a suicide.

2020 – today

Aftermath

Ghislaine Maxwell sentenced to 20 years, compensation for victims from banks and a dedicated fund, then a political battle over releasing the files.

Justice and victims

Dozens of women, many of them minors at the time, came forward against Epstein. His death ended the criminal case against him, but other proceedings continued:

  • Ghislaine Maxwell — sentenced to 20 years in prison; her appeals, including to the Supreme Court, have been rejected.
  • Compensation — a fund paid for by Epstein's estate compensated more than a hundred victims; banks also reached settlements.
  • The 2008 deal — a 2020 Justice Department report found that the prosecutor at the time showed “poor judgment”.
  • His death — a 2023 inspector general report found serious negligence by prison guards.

Questions still open

His death

  • The official finding (medical examiner, FBI, Justice Department) is suicide.
  • Failures in supervision (cameras, guards) fuelled doubts and many theories.
  • No official investigation has concluded it was a homicide.

His network

  • Who knew, and who may have taken part? Apart from Maxwell, no one else has been charged in the US.
  • Why was the 2008 deal so lenient?
  • What the unreleased pages contain remains a political battle.
