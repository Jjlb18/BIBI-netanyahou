Joseph Stalin

Leader of the Soviet Union from Lenin’s death (1924) until his own in 1953. He made the USSR a superpower, at the cost of purges, famines and the Gulag camps that killed millions.

Stalinism

Absolute power. One party, a political police (NKVD), censorship and a personality cult presenting Stalin as the “father of the peoples”.

Industrialise at any cost. Five‑year plans, giant factories and forced collectivisation of farmland, at the cost of immense suffering.

Biography

Early years

Son of a Georgian cobbler, he studied at the Tiflis (Tbilisi) seminary, then became a revolutionary, arrested and exiled to Siberia several times.

Revolution

Close to Lenin, he took part in the 1917 Bolshevik revolution and became the party’s general secretary in 1922.

Taking power

After Lenin died in 1924, he pushed aside his rivals one by one, including Trotsky, and was sole ruler by the end of the 1920s.

End

He died of a stroke on 5 March 1953. In 1956 his successor Khrushchev denounced his crimes and his personality cult.

In power

1929 – 1933

Collectivisation

Peasants were forced into collective farms. The 1932–33 famine killed millions, notably in Ukraine (the Holodomor).

1936 – 1938

The Great Terror

Show trials and mass executions: hundreds of thousands shot and millions sent to the Gulag.

1939 – 1953

War and Cold War

A pact with Hitler (1939), then war against Nazi Germany (1941–45), won at an enormous human cost. Domination of Eastern Europe afterwards.

Crimes

The Stalinist regime is responsible for the deaths of millions of people.

  • Famines — Collectivisation caused the 1932–33 famine, which killed millions.
  • The Terror — Purges of the party, the army and the population, with mass executions in 1937–38.
  • The Gulag — A vast network of forced‑labour camps through which millions passed and where many died.
  • Deportations — Entire peoples (Chechens, Crimean Tatars…) deported to Central Asia and Siberia.

Legacy and memory

Human cost

  • Millions dead in famines, purges and camps.
  • Entire peoples deported.
  • All opposition crushed for nearly thirty years.

How he is remembered today

  • His role in defeating Nazism is still highlighted in Russia, where his image has been partly rehabilitated.
  • In Ukraine, the Holodomor is commemorated as a genocide.
  • Historians and the NGO Memorial have documented the victims of repression.
