Adolf Hitler

Leader of the Nazi Party and dictator of Germany from 1933 to 1945. Responsible for the Second World War in Europe and the Holocaust, the murder of six million Jews.

Nazi ideology

Racism and antisemitism. Nazism was built on the idea of a superior “race” and named Jews as enemies to be destroyed. Roma, disabled people, gay men and political opponents were also persecuted.

Dictatorship and conquest. A single party, propaganda, police terror (Gestapo, SS) and the goal of conquering “living space” in the East.

Biography

Early years

Born in Austria, son of a customs official, he failed to get into the Vienna art academy and lived on the margins. He moved to Munich in 1913.

World War I

A soldier in the Bavarian army, he saw the 1918 defeat as a humiliation and then joined a small far‑right party.

Rise

Leader of the Nazi Party from 1921, he led a failed coup in Munich in 1923 and wrote Mein Kampf in prison. The 1929 crash made his party surge at the polls.

End

He killed himself in his Berlin bunker on 30 April 1945, with Eva Braun, as the Red Army took the city.

In power

Jan 1933

Seizing power

Appointed chancellor, within months he abolished freedoms, parties and trade unions. In 1934 he took all powers.

1935 – 1939

Persecution and rearmament

Nuremberg Laws against Jews (1935), the “Kristallnacht” pogrom (1938), annexation of Austria and Czechoslovakia.

1939 – 1945

War and genocide

Invasion of Poland, Western Europe and then the USSR. Murder of Europe’s Jews in ghettos and camps such as Auschwitz.

Crimes

Hitler’s regime committed mass crimes on an unprecedented scale.

  • The Holocaust — About six million Jews murdered — two thirds of Europe’s Jews.
  • Other victims — Hundreds of thousands of Roma, disabled people (the so‑called “euthanasia” programme), opponents and Soviet prisoners of war.
  • The war — The Second World War he started killed tens of millions of people in Europe.
  • Nuremberg trials — Dead before the war ended, he was never tried; his main accomplices were, in 1945–46.

Legacy and memory

Human cost

  • Six million Jews murdered.
  • Tens of millions dead in the war in Europe.
  • Whole cities and countries destroyed.

How he is remembered today

  • Nazi symbols and Holocaust denial are banned in several countries, including Germany and France.
  • Former camps such as Auschwitz are now memorial sites.
  • He remains the most studied example of dictatorship and genocide.
