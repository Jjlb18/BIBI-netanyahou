Fascism

A new ideology. Mussolini invented fascism after 1918: extreme nationalism, a cult of the leader, rejection of democracy and communism, and the violence of his “Blackshirt” militias.

A model for others. Italian fascism inspired other dictators, including Hitler and Franco.

Biography

Early years

Son of a socialist blacksmith and a schoolteacher, he became a teacher himself, then a journalist and socialist activist, notably in Switzerland.

Break

Expelled from the Socialist Party in 1914 for wanting Italy to join the war, he founded his own newspaper and fought at the front.

Rise

He created the Fasci di Combattimento in 1919. After the “March on Rome” in October 1922, the king made him head of government.

End

Overthrown in 1943, he then ran a German‑protected puppet state. In April 1945 he was caught fleeing and shot by partisans; his body was displayed in Milan.

In power

1922 – 1925

Building the dictatorship

After the murder of socialist MP Matteotti (1924), he abolished parties, the free press and elections.

1935 – 1938

War and racial laws

Invasion of Ethiopia (1935–36), with the use of poison gas, then antisemitic laws in 1938.

1939 – 1945

Beside Hitler

Pact of Steel (1939), entry into the war in 1940, defeats, arrest in July 1943, then the Salò Republic until his death.

Crimes

The fascist regime used violence both in Italy and abroad.

  • Political violence — Militias, murders of opponents (including Giacomo Matteotti), prison and internal exile for adversaries.
  • Colonial wars — Bloody repression in Libya and use of chemical weapons in Ethiopia.
  • Racial laws — From 1938 Italian Jews were excluded from schools, the army and many jobs; under Salò, thousands were deported to Nazi camps.
  • The war — The alliance with Hitler led Italy to military disaster, occupation and civil war.

Legacy and memory

Human cost

  • Opponents murdered, jailed or exiled.
  • Tens of thousands dead in colonial wars.
  • Thousands of Italian Jews deported.

How he is remembered today

  • Glorifying fascism is banned by law in Italy.
  • His tomb in Predappio still draws sympathisers, which is controversial.
  • On 25 April, Italy celebrates its Liberation from fascism and Nazi occupation.
